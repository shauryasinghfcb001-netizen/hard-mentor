  note: The force between two point charges q1 and q2 a distance r apart is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to r squared: F = k |q1 q2| / r^2, acting along the line joining them. In SI units k = 1/(4 pi epsilon_0), about 9 x 10^9 N m^2 C^-2, and the permittivity of free space epsilon_0 is about 8.854 x 10^-12 C^2 N^-1 m^-2. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract.

  note: For several charges, the force on any one charge is the vector sum of the forces from each of the other charges, each found with Coulomb's law as if the others were absent. The presence of other charges does not change an individual pairwise force. This is the principle of superposition.

Q: What is the direction of the electric field due to a positive point charge?

  Why this note: it has the question's word pairs "electric field", "positive charge", "point charge" close together; it contains direction, electric, field, positive, point, charge.
  gate: best phy12-1-8 score 7.41, supports 100% of key terms; states together: "electric field", "positive charge", "point charge"
  note: The electric field at a point is the force per unit positive test charge placed there, E = F/q. For a point source charge Q it has magnitude Q/(4 pi epsilon_0 r^2) and points radially away from a positive charge. The test charge must be very small so it does not disturb the source. The field of a system of charges is the vector sum of the fields of each charge (superposition).

Q: Need the formula for electric field of a line charge

  note: Infinitely long straight wire with linear charge density lambda: E = lambda/(2 pi epsilon_0 r), radial, outward for positive lambda. Infinite plane sheet with surface charge density sigma: E = sigma/(2 epsilon_0), normal to the sheet and independent of distance. Uniformly charged thin spherical shell: outside, the field is as if the whole charge sat at the centre; inside, the field is zero at every point.

Q: Why do like charges push each other away?
  ANSWERED from phy12-1-2 (NCERT Physics Part I, Class 12, Ch 1, §1.2, pp. 1-2: Electric charge: two kinds, like repel and unlike attract)
  Why this note: it has the question's word pairs "like push", "charges push" close together; it contains like, charges, push; not in it: other.
  gate: best phy12-1-2 score 4.90, supports 59% of key terms; states together: "like push", "charges push"
  note: Rubbing materials together electrifies them. There are only two kinds of charge: like charges repel and unlike charges attract. The property that separates the two kinds is called the polarity of charge. By convention, the charge on a glass rod or cat's fur is called positive and the charge on a plastic rod or silk is called negative. Rubbing a glass rod with silk moves electrons from the rod to the silk; no new charge is created.
